# Customer interview guide

Drafted Oct 1, 2026 for Phase 0 (Oct 5 – Oct 16). Goal: 15–20 interviews, enough to pick the launch niche and pass Gate 0.

## What we need to learn

Every question below serves one of these. If an answer doesn't move one of them, move on.

| # | Question we must answer | Decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Who feels the pain of getting software built most often and most sharply? | Launch niche |
| 2 | How do they get software built today, and what does it cost them (money, time, stress)? | Our real competition and price ceiling |
| 3 | What goes wrong? Is lack of visibility and control a top pain, or a minor one? | Whether the "live office + approvals" pitch lands |
| 4 | Have they tried AI app builders (Lovable, Bolt, Replit, cto.new)? Where did those fall short? | Our differentiation |
| 5 | Do they care about owning the code and accounts? | How much to invest in ownership features |
| 6 | Would they pay, how much, and how (subscription, per app, per hour of expert help)? | Pricing and plans |
| 7 | Will they commit to something now (beta slot, intro, pre-payment)? | Gate 0 |

## Rules for every interview

Based on *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick): people are polite about ideas, but honest about their past.

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time you…" beats "Would you use…?"
2. **Don't pitch until the last 10 minutes.** Once they've heard the idea, everything they say about their problems is coloured by it.
3. **Get specifics:** names of tools, amounts of money, dates, number of weeks. "It was expensive" → "Roughly how much?"
4. **Dig into emotion:** "That sounds frustrating, what happened next?" Strong pain shows up as stories, not adjectives.
5. **Compliments are not data.** "Cool idea!" means nothing. Money, time and introductions are data.
6. **Talk less than 30% of the time.** Silence is fine; let them fill it.
7. **Always end with an ask** (beta, intro, pre-payment). Their answer is the most honest data of the interview.

## Who to interview

Aim for 6–7 per segment so we can compare.

| Segment | Must be true | Where to find them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Agencies** (recommended niche) | Runs or leads a small agency (2–30 people) that builds apps, websites or internal tools for clients; has outsourced or hired developers in the last 12 months | LinkedIn, Clutch, agency founder communities, Upwork top agencies, local tech meetups |
| **B. Non-technical founders** | Has a software idea or early product; can't code; has tried to get it built (freelancer, agency, no-code, AI builder) in the last 12 months | Indie Hackers, startup communities, incubators, Product Hunt, founder WhatsApp/Slack groups |
| **C. Small businesses** | Owner or ops lead of a 5–100 person business that wanted a custom internal tool (booking, CRM, dashboard) in the last 12 months | Local business networks, salons/clinics/coaching centres, small manufacturers, your own network |

**Screener (ask before booking):** "In the last 12 months, have you tried to get a piece of software built, by hiring someone, using an agency, or using a tool?" If no, skip them; they can only speculate.

**Booking message (adapt):**

> Hi {name}, I'm researching how {agencies / founders / small businesses} get custom software built today, and what goes wrong. I'm not selling anything; I'd just like to hear about your experience. Would you have 30 minutes this week or next? Happy to share what I learn across all the conversations.

## Interview script (about 40 minutes)

### 1. Opening (2 min)

- Thank them; confirm 30–40 minutes.
- "I'm trying to understand how people get software built and where it gets painful. There are no right answers; I'm here to learn, not to sell."
- Ask permission to take notes or record.

### 2. Their context (5 min)

1. Tell me a bit about your {business / agency / startup}. What do you do, and who for?
2. Where does software fit in: something you sell, something you run the business on, or both?
3. Roughly how many software projects have you been involved in over the last year?

### 3. The last project, as a story (12 min)

The core of the interview. Pick their most recent software project and walk through it in order.

4. Tell me about the most recent piece of software you got built. What was it, and why did you need it?
5. How did you decide who or what would build it? What else did you consider?
6. Walk me through what happened, from the idea to it being live (or abandoned).
7. How long did it take, compared with what you expected?
8. What did it cost in total? (Developers, agency, tools, your own time.)
9. What was the hardest or most frustrating moment? What did you do about it?
10. At any point, did you not know what was going on, or whether it was on track? How did you find out?
11. How did you judge whether the work was good? Who checked it?
12. Is it still running today? Who maintains it, and what happens when something breaks or you want a change?

### 4. Current solutions and spending (6 min)

13. Which tools or people do you use for building software today? (Freelancers, agencies, in-house, no-code, AI builders.)
14. Roughly how much do you spend on getting software built in a year?
15. If you could change one thing about how you get software built, what would it be?

### 5. AI tools (5 min)

16. Have you tried any AI tools to build software, like ChatGPT, Lovable, Bolt, Replit, v0 or cto.new? Which ones?
17. What did you try to build? How far did you get?
18. Where did it break down, or why did you stop?
19. *(If they haven't tried any)* What's stopped you?

### 6. Ownership and control (3 min)

20. Who owns the code and the accounts (hosting, database, domain) for your last project? Has that ever caused a problem?
21. When someone builds software for you, how involved do you want to be? Which decisions do you want to make yourself?

### 7. Concept reaction (7 min). Only now show the idea.

Describe it in two sentences, then show the office mock-up if we have it:

> "Imagine an AI software company: a product manager, tech lead, developers, QA and DevOps, all AI agents. You describe what you need, approve the plan, watch them work in a live office, and approve the release. You own the code."

22. What's your first reaction?
23. What would you use it for first? *(Get a concrete project.)*
24. What would worry you, or stop you from trying it?
25. What would it need to do for you to trust it with a real project?
26. Which part matters most to you: watching the work, approving the key steps, owning the code, or the price?

### 8. Pricing (4 min)

Use local currency. Ask in this order, then compare with what they spend today (question 14).

27. At what price per month would this be so cheap you'd doubt the quality?
28. At what price would it feel like a bargain?
29. At what price would it start to feel expensive, but you'd still consider it?
30. At what price would it be too expensive to consider?
31. Would you rather pay monthly, per app built, or per hour of real-engineer help when the agents get stuck?

### 9. Close with an ask (3 min)

32. **The commitment ask (always):** "We're starting a small private beta with 10–20 {agencies / founders / businesses} in January. Would you like a spot?" If yes: "Would you be willing to {pay a small deposit / commit to building one real project / give us 30 minutes of feedback each week}?"
33. Who else should I talk to? Could you introduce me?
34. Can I follow up when we have something to show you?

## Extra questions per segment

Swap these in for questions 2–3 or 13–15 where they fit.

**A. Agencies**
- How do you staff a new client project today? What happens when you're short of developers?
- What's your typical project size and margin? Where does the margin get eaten?
- How do you report progress to clients? How much time does that take?
- Would you put a tool like this in front of your clients (white-labelled), or keep it behind the scenes?

**B. Non-technical founders**
- How did you try to validate the idea before building?
- Have you tried to find a technical co-founder? What happened?
- How did you choose between a freelancer, an agency and building it yourself with tools?

**C. Small businesses**
- What do you use today instead (spreadsheets, WhatsApp, paper, off-the-shelf software)? What's wrong with it?
- Who would use the tool day to day, and how comfortable are they with software?
- Who decides on spending like this, and what would they need to see?

## Scoring and Gate 0

Score each interview right after writing the notes.

| Score | Pain | Spend | Fit | Commitment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | No real problem | Spends nothing | Needs something we won't build (mobile app, hardware) | Declined everything |
| 1 | Mild annoyance | Small one-off amounts | Partly fits | Follow-up only |
| 2 | Real problem, has workarounds | Regular spend | Fits a web app we can build | Beta spot |
| 3 | Urgent, tells stories unprompted | Large or growing spend | Exactly our first use case | Deposit, real project or introductions |

**Gate 0 reads the commitment column, not opinions.** "Would pay" counts only with a commitment score of 2 or more. Pass: at least 5 such people, ideally concentrated in one segment.

**Picking the niche:** after all interviews, compare the segments on average pain, spend and commitment scores. Pick the segment with the most commitment-2+ people, then the highest spend. Write the decision and its reasons in the roadmap.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Asking "Would you use this?" or "Would you pay for this?" Everyone says yes. Ask about the past, and ask for a commitment.
- Pitching early, or defending the idea when they criticise it. Criticism is the most useful part; ask "why?"
- Interviewing only friends. Include at least half strangers from the target segments.
- Leading questions ("Isn't it frustrating when…?"). Ask open ones ("What was that like?").
- Taking notes as conclusions ("They love it"). Write what they said and did.
